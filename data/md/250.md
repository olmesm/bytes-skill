### 2024 predictions that you can take to the bank

1. **Bun achieves their goal of becoming the default runtime for the frontend.** They still have some hurdles to jump over, but if they can succeed in providing a drop-in replacement for Node that instantly improves your app’s performance by 10x, it’ll be a no-brainer choice for most developers. Last September’s v1.0 release was a major step towards Windows support and general stability, and we’re betting that Bun starts becoming the default choice this year.
2. **More than three of your favorite OSS/devtool startups will die.** We’re not naming names, but if they’ve been around for 2+ years and aren’t profitably growing revenue, they’re in the danger zone. Though some may stick around a little longer by becoming a zombie startup where the founder fires most of the team and lives the VC growth company as a solo indie hacker dream – it’s not a great outcome for anyone.
3. **AI will replace no-code/low-code tools.** It turns out that AI is a lot better and faster at building analytics dashboards than Jim from marketing. Tools like [Basedash](https://www.basedash.com/) already use AI to build you a full suite of custom internal tools, and others will emerge to replace the drag-and-drop builders for creating sites that don’t depend heavily on business logic.
4. **Netlify gets acquired by GoDaddy.** With multiple rounds of layoffs, 2023 clearly wasn’t the best year for Netlify. But sometimes the best way to rebound is to find yourself a new ~~sugar daddy~~ GoDaddy. After retiring the Media Temple brand a few months ago, it feels like GoDaddy could be back in the acquisition market for a platform like Netlify.
5. **Everyone will actually figure out how to build apps with RSCs** – without needing to scour random Twitter threads. It took a few years longer than expected, but that’s the status quo in the React ecosystem And speaking of things taking years longer than expected…
6. **React forget will *not* ship.** We first wrote about React Forget, React’s experimental memoization compiler, in [issue #78](https://bytes.dev/archives/78) of Bytes. Though we’ve heard rumors that they’re already using it at Meta, it’s a hard problem to solve and we remain doubtful it’ll actually ship in 2024. The key to happiness is low expectations, after all.
7. **No one will figure out what “Local First” actually means.** Is it about optimistic updates? Data sovereignty? A new server/client paradigm? Network agnostic data stores? All of those things? We’re still not totally sure, but we’ll keep trying in 2024.
8. **2024 is the year of ~~Linux on the Desktop~~ data on the edge.** In what may be perceived as a contradiction of prediction #7 (again, we truly don’t know), lead by tech like Turso, LiteFs, and Cloudflare D1, 2024 will be the year we finally start moving our data closer to our users.

**Bottom Line:** I’m not sure if FanDuel and the other gambling sites let you bet on annual JavaScript predictions yet, but if they do, you might want to go lock these in now. I can’t think of a better investment for 2024.

## Cool Bits

1. Mayank wrote an article called [React Server Components: the Good, the Bad, and the Ugly](https://www.mayank.co/blog/react-server-components/). I’ve read it twice, and I’m still not sure if Next.js is supposed to be Clint Eastwood or the guy who plays Tuco.
2. Colin Campbell wrote about [Concurrent React, External Stores, and Tearing](https://interbolt.org/blog/react-ui-tearing/).
3. Check out your [2023 Year in code](https://year-in-code.com/). Created by the Graphite team, it gives you a personalized *Spotify Wrapped*-style video for all your GitHub stats. I just learned that I’m in the top 1% of developers who use `!important`. Checks out. [sponsored]
4. [date-fns v3](https://blog.date-fns.org/v3-is-out/) was just released and is now 100% TypeScript and comes with ESM support for Node. It’s also up to 70 million downloads a month now.
5. Chris Rybicki wrote about [Why JavaScript promises aren’t technically monads](https://rybicki.io/blog/2023/12/23/promises-arent-monads.html), which means I owe my stepmom an apology for a long fight we got into on Christmas morning.
6. [jQuery 4.0](https://github.com/jquery/jquery/issues/5365) is coming soon to a theater near you.
7. Ben Visness wrote an article called [I made JSX for Lua (because I hate static sites)](https://bvisness.me/luax/). Cover your ears, Netlify, he doesn’t mean it.
8. V8 introduced a fast, new optimizing compiler called [Maglev](https://v8.dev/blog/maglev).
9. Glauber Costa dared to ask, [Is NoSQL dead?](https://blog.turso.tech/is-nosql-dead-fe57c5e4) Regardless of the answer, it’s usually not a *great* sign when people start writing these types of articles about you.
10. Simon Willison wrote about [Stuff we figured out about AI in 2023](https://simonwillison.net/2023/Dec/31/ai-in-2023/). I’m a little surprised that people aren’t more worried about the part where AI “acquired a taste for human flesh,” but I guess we’re all busy.
