# Guardrails

The dial is set to full Bytes: irreverent, self-mocking, happy to puncture hype. That is a setting for *tone*. The lines below are about *targets and topics*, and they hold at any setting, because this goes to thousands of colleagues in many countries and gets forwarded to people who were not the intended audience.

Bytes itself crosses these lines now and then (a crossed-out "terrorists" for GraphQL developers, a caption about impressing "the ladies"). Those work for an opt-in audience of strangers. They do not work in a company inbox. Copy the craft, not those.

## Never the punchline
- A named or identifiable colleague, team, office, country or customer.
- Nationality, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexuality, age, disability, health, body, accent or language ability.
- Politics, elections, wars, disasters, deaths, crime, or any news story with victims.
- Layoffs, reorganisations, pay, performance, hiring freezes, or the company's financial results.
- Competitors and vendors by name. (The industry in general is fine: "another JavaScript framework" yes, a named rival no.)
- Alcohol, drugs, sex, profanity. "wtf" in a headline is the ceiling.

## Never in the issue at all
- Security fixes, vulnerabilities, incident details or anything under embargo, even described vaguely.
- Unreleased products, customer names, contract or revenue figures, legal matters.
- Secrets, internal hostnames, credentials or personal data, including inside screenshots. Check every pixel of every screenshot.
- Anything from a private repository the whole readership cannot already see, unless the user confirms it.
- Blame. An outage or a revert can be covered only as a fix someone shipped, with their agreement implied by a public PR, and never with a culprit.

## The checks
Run these on each joke in the finished draft:

1. **Who is the target?** If it is a person or a group of people other than the narrator, rewrite it so the target is a thing.
2. **The forwarding test.** Would it be fine pasted, alone and without context, into a message to the CEO, a customer, and a journalist?
3. **The translation test.** Does it still make sense, and is it still harmless, read literally by someone in another country?
4. **The subject test.** Would the person whose work is being described be pleased to see this next to their name?
5. **The fact test.** Is the fact under the joke still accurate, or did the exaggeration change what a reader would believe happened?

A joke that fails gets replaced or removed. A plain sentence is always an acceptable substitute, and an issue with eight good jokes is better than one with twelve and an apology.

## When unsure
Keep the line in the draft and flag it in the hand-over summary ("not sure about the tail on Cool Bit 4"). The reviewer decides. Do not silently water the whole issue down to avoid the question: bland is also a failure, because nobody reads the next one.
